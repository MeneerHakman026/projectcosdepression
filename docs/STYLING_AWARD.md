# COS-D — Styling & Design Direction

## Design ambition

COS-D should not merely be a functional academic research website.

The ambition is to create a distinctive, highly accessible research website that communicates a serious scientific project in a human, contemporary and memorable way.

The website is intended to be submitted to **The Academic Designer website awards in 2027**.

This is not a reason to over-design the website. It raises the quality bar.

Every design decision should strengthen:
1. understanding,
2. trust,
3. participation,
4. accessibility,
5. COS-D's own visual identity.

---

## Core design principle

> Every distinctive design choice should communicate something about COS-D.

Avoid decoration for decoration's sake.

COS-D brings together three perspectives:

- people with depression,
- healthcare professionals,
- informal caregivers.

These perspectives come together to decide what matters in depression research and care.

The three-part visual language of the COS-D identity should therefore be used meaningfully throughout the website.

The three colours are not simply decorative brand colours. They represent the collaborative nature of the project.

---

## Desired feeling

The website should feel:

- human
- calm
- credible
- contemporary
- welcoming
- thoughtful
- accessible
- editorial
- research-led
- understated but distinctive

It should be serious without feeling institutional or cold.

It should be visually sophisticated without looking like a technology startup, SaaS product or marketing landing page.

---

## Visual identity

Primary COS-D palette:

- Teal: `#4f9aa7`
- Mint: `#9fd0c2`
- Navy: `#1c2c43`
- Neutral/off-white backgrounds where appropriate

Use navy as the primary dark/text colour.

Use the three COS-D colours deliberately and consistently.

For accessibility, adjusted/deeper variants may be used where the original brand colour does not provide sufficient contrast for text or interactive elements.

Accessibility takes precedence over exact colour reproduction.

---

## The three-perspective visual language

The COS-D logo represents three target groups coming together.

This concept should subtly influence the broader design system.

Possible expressions include:

- the existing three-segment TriMark
- three-part rules or dividers
- colour-coded participant groups
- structured three-part compositions
- subtle visual convergence
- photography or content that represents multiple perspectives

Do not repeatedly draw literal tears/drops everywhere.

The identity should feel coherent rather than themed.

---

## Typography

Typography should be:

- highly readable
- contemporary
- confident
- restrained

Large headings may be expressive and bold, especially on the homepage.

Body copy should remain comfortable to read and use sensible line lengths.

Avoid overly academic typography conventions simply to make the project look scholarly.

The science provides the academic credibility; the typography should make it understandable.

---

## Layout

Use generous whitespace, but whitespace must feel intentional.

Avoid:
- tiny content islands on large screens
- unnecessarily narrow layouts on ultrawide displays
- huge empty hero areas without compositional purpose
- excessive cards and containers
- dashboard-like grids

Prefer:
- editorial composition
- strong hierarchy
- asymmetric layouts where appropriate
- large purposeful imagery
- readable text columns
- progressive responsive scaling

The layout should adapt intentionally across:
- mobile (~375–430px)
- tablet
- normal desktop
- large desktop
- ultrawide displays

Mobile is a first-class experience, not a stacked desktop layout.

---

## Photography

Photography will be an important part of making COS-D feel human.

Preferred direction:

- documentary/editorial
- natural light
- authentic environments
- human and understated
- diverse without looking artificially assembled
- emotionally respectful

Avoid stereotypical mental-health stock photography:
- staged sadness
- person holding their head in their hands
- exaggerated clinical imagery
- smiling doctor with clipboard
- generic corporate team photography

Photography should communicate people and participation rather than illustrate "depression" literally.

Where possible, use genuine project/team/community photography with appropriate consent.

---

## Interaction

Interaction should be restrained.

Use motion only when it improves:
- orientation
- hierarchy
- comprehension
- continuity

Avoid:
- gamification
- unnecessary parallax
- flashy transitions
- animation for decoration
- pop-ups
- attention-seeking effects

The website should feel alive, not animated.

---

## Participation

The survey is the primary user action.

"Take the survey" should remain clearly visible throughout the experience without making the website feel commercial or conversion-driven.

The participant journey should broadly be:

Arrival
→ Understand COS-D
→ Understand why it matters
→ Recognise the people involved
→ Build trust
→ See that others are participating
→ Explore further if desired
→ Participate

Participation should feel like contributing to research, not clicking a marketing CTA.

---

## Content design

Research content should be translated into clear language without oversimplifying its meaning.

Whenever possible:

- explain why something matters before explaining methodology
- provide context around research outputs
- use plain language
- progressively disclose academic detail
- make terminology understandable
- connect publications and research activity to the larger COS-D story

The Depression Dictionary can become an important accessibility and science-communication feature.

---

## Accessibility

Accessibility is a core design criterion, not a final compliance task.

Design and implementation should consider:

- WCAG contrast
- keyboard navigation
- visible focus states
- semantic HTML
- heading hierarchy
- descriptive links
- meaningful alt text
- reduced-motion preferences
- responsive text
- touch target sizes
- screen-reader compatibility
- readable line lengths
- understandable language

Do not sacrifice accessibility for aesthetics.

A visually distinctive design that excludes users is not successful COS-D design.

---

## 2027 award benchmark

COS-D is intended for submission to **The Academic Designer website awards in 2027**.

The 2025 winners are a design benchmark, not templates to copy.

Reference:
https://theacademicdesigner.com/2025/winners-of-the-best-personal-academic-websites-contest-2025/#hira-javed

Lessons taken from the 2025 jury examples include:

- strong visual cohesion matters
- typography, colour, illustration and imagery should feel like one system
- research should be communicated as a story rather than a publication dump
- authentic imagery can substantially improve academic websites
- complex research should be understandable to non-specialists
- accessibility matters alongside aesthetics
- visual elements should strengthen communication rather than merely decorate it
- a website does not need to be enormous to be excellent

Do not copy the visual style of previous winners.

COS-D should be recognisable as COS-D.

---

## Quality test

Before considering a major design element finished, ask:

### Participant
"Can I understand what this project is, why it matters, and what I can do?"

### Chris / research team
"Does this accurately represent our research and feel like our project?"

### Researcher
"Does this feel credible and scientifically serious?"

### Designer
"Does this have a coherent and recognisable visual language?"

### Accessibility
"Can people actually use and understand this?"

### Award jury
"Is there a clear idea here that I will remember after seeing twenty other academic websites?"

If an element only succeeds at the final question and fails the others, it should not be included.

---

## Anti-goals

COS-D should NOT become:

- a generic university template
- a corporate healthcare website
- a SaaS landing page
- a portfolio website
- a collection of cards
- an animation showcase
- an academic wall of text
- an award-bait design experiment

The strongest version of COS-D is simple, human, scientifically credible and unmistakably its own.
